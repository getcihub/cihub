# Analytics: Postgres first, ClickHouse later

Analytics goes through a single trait, `AnalyticsStore` (`cihub-analytics`), with two implementations: Postgres, the only one shipped in v1 and sufficient up to a few million events per month, and ClickHouse, enabled by configuration when the volume or the depth of history justifies it. The notifier feeds one or the other from the log `events`, the modules see only the interface, and the dashboard asks its questions to the interface. Moving from one to the other is a data migration, not a change of application code.

In v1 (lot 11a), the Postgres implementation derives four fact tables from the event stream, indexed by queue and date: `an_item_conclusions` (one row per concluded item: its conclusion, its reason, its submission and its end), `an_state_durations` (one row per pass of a concluded item through a state), `an_test_run_results` (one row per ended run: its group, its attempt, its status and its reason) and `an_check_results` (the last report of each check on the test commit of an ended run, and whether it passed after it failed on the attempt before). They are not partitioned yet and keep every row until lot 15.

The Health reads only the hourly aggregate of each queue, so that its cost follows the length of a period, not the number of PRs: three tables keyed by queue and UTC hour, which the notifier adds to in the transaction of the facts, from the facts that it writes only, so that a log read again counts once. `an_hourly_conclusions` counts the ended items by conclusion and reason; `an_hourly_durations` holds a histogram of the time to merge and of the time in each live state of the merged PRs, in buckets 10 % wider each, so that a median or a 90th percentile read from it is within 5 % of the exact one; `an_hourly_checks` counts per check its runs, its failures and its passes on a retry. Three typed tables rather than the one `measurements` table of `specs/SCHEMA_PLAN.md`: a reason, a check and a bucket stay columns of their own type (decided October 15, 2026, by the project lead). On a queue of 30,000 PRs a day, the Health of 30 days took 37 s read from the facts, and takes 0.1 s read from the aggregate. The ClickHouse schema below is the equivalent target, column for column, so that the migration is a copy.

In both implementations, the notifier writes only from the log `events`, after its cursor per queue (`log_cursors`), and reads no projection: at the end of an item, it reads the transitions of the item from the log (index on `item_id`); at the end of a run, its creation, its assembly and the reports of the checks on its test commit between the two. A replay of the log therefore writes the same facts. With Postgres, the write of the facts and the advance of the cursor are in the same transaction: exactly-once is free. With ClickHouse, the notifier reserves a deterministic batch (`batch_id`, up to 1,000 rows or 2 s), inserts it with `insert_deduplication_token = batch_id` so that a recovery after a crash is discarded rather than counted twice, advances the cursor only after acknowledgment, and the queries read the `ReplacingMergeTree` tables with `FINAL` or `argMax` because the replacement is deferred.

Questions the schema answers directly, each matching a chart of the dashboard or an API call:

- Number of conclusions per day and per queue, broken down by reason (histogram of the Health view; `item_conclusions`).
- Time in queue p50/p95/p99 per day or per hour (curve of the Health view; `quantileExact` on `item_conclusions`, or the aggregate for ranges beyond 30 days).
- Test duration and CI runs triggered per PR (`test_run_results`), including the share of bisection.
- Restart rate and its causes (`state_durations` whose `exit_reason` is a restart reason), the first thing to look at when a queue seems slow.
- Throughput per impacted target in parallel mode (`arrayJoin(impacted_targets)`).
- Detail of the PRs behind each bar (capped at 2,500 rows).

The Prometheus endpoint (`GET /api/v1/metrics`) is served from Postgres: instant gauges (`mq_depth_current`, `mq_awaiting_mergeability`, `mq_testing_slots_active`, `mq_prs_testing`) from the live tables, sliding windows (`mq_pr_conclusions_1h_total`, `mq_pr_restarts_1h_total`, `mq_pr_state_duration_1h_seconds{state}` and the histograms of wait, test and time in queue with the buckets 60, 300, 600, 900, 1800, 3600, 5400, 7200 s) from the fact tables, labeled by `repo`, `branch` and `queue_type` (`main`, `bisection`). The totals are also exposed as true counters (`mq_pr_conclusions_total`), so that `rate()` works.

Retention with Postgres: 90 days for `events`; the fact tables keep every row until lot 15, which partitions them by month and keeps 13 months, detached by a daily job; unlimited for the hourly aggregate. With ClickHouse: 13 months for `events`, unlimited for the aggregates, one replica per instance with `ReplicatedMergeTree` as soon as a second node exists. In both cases, the query layer applies the same isolation by `org_id` as the rest of the database.
